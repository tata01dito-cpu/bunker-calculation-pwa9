Total consumption

How the results are calculated

Arrival ROB is the ROB after the preceding sea passage. Departure ROB is after current port consumption plus any bunker credited at that port. Consumable fuel tanks are switched automatically in the selected sequence when the active tank is exhausted. No negative ROB is carried; a shortage is flagged instead.

Inputs

Present position, position status, per-tank sequence and display order, tank role (storage/settling/service/support) and in-service tank selection, tank capacity/ROB/unpumpable quantity, fuel oil type (VLSFO/HFO) for mixing rules, explicit bunkering, port UTC offset, separate ETA/ETD, lubricant rates and reserve basis.

Hints & how to use the planner

  1. Setup first. Enter vessel, present position with its UTC offset, noon report date (local time), fuel/lubricant consumption rates and reserve basis.
  2. Configure Tanks second. Enter every relevant tank, capacity, present ROB, unpumpable quantity and consumption sequence. Mark tanks that are actually in service for automatic consumption.
  3. Voyage / Ports third. Add ports in sailing order. Enter distance, speed, port stay in days + hours and UTC offset.
  4. Bunkering & Lube Oil, and ECA/SECA. Plan fuel bunkering and lube oil replenishment (with ETA/ETD tank-condition bars) in the Bunkering tab, and any Emission Control Area fuel changeover in the ECA/SECA tab.
  5. Plan a bunkering. On the Dashboard, tap Plan a bunkering, choose a port (or another port with its arrival date) to see how much fuel, diesel and lube oil to take, tank by tank. You can change the grade, sulphur or any tank quantity, export a Word file, or write the plan into the Bunkering tab.
  6. Review the Dashboard. Tap any route point to inspect ETA/ETD, sea and port consumption, fuel ROB and tank-by-tank ROB at ETA → ETD.
  7. Save your work. In Results, save the voyage under a name to reopen it anytime on this device. Everything recalculates automatically as you type — there is no Recalculate button.
  8. Need the explanations? Tap or focus any input to reveal its hint text, or switch on “Show all field hints on screen” above to show every hint at once.
  9. Alerts. Tap the bell icon, then tap any alert — the app jumps to the field that causes it and shows how to correct it.
  10. Calculator. The calculator icon in the top bar opens a built-in calculator.
ETA vs voyage start

The noon report date (local time at the present position, converted with the UTC offset beside Present position) is the time reference for the plan. The first port ETA is an arrival time. If the first port is the departure/current port and distance is 0 NM, they can be the same. If the first port represents the next destination, its ETA should include the first sailing leg.

    About Bunker Pilot

    Version 1.62

    Program design and creator: CE Noel M. Tobongbanua — Chief Marine Engineer, Mechanical Engineer, Maritime Instructor. Owner/developer of the e-ORB Marine mobile application, and other maritime software and training tools.

    The app is designed to support shipboard engineers, chief engineers, marine engineering students, cadets, maritime instructors and technical personnel with practical bunker, ROB, tank, consumption and voyage-planning workflows.

    The calculator supports planning and documentation. It does not replace the vessel's approved procedures, SMS, tank calibration tables, bunker documentation, maker instructions or Flag/Class requirements.
